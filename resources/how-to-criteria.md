# How-to guides verification criteria

1. The title clearly expresses a specific practical goal that the user can accomplish.
1. The title and opening paragraph describe the same goal, without shifting scope or introducing a different task.
1. The guide begins with a clear statement of intent explaining what the user will achieve.
1. The guide has a recognizable procedural structure, such as numbered steps, action-oriented headings, or clearly ordered sections.
1. The guide follows a logical sequence from starting condition to completed outcome.
1. Each major step tells the user what to do, rather than merely describing what is possible.There can be some conditions and branching.
1. Step text and headings use imperative or action-oriented language where appropriate.
1. The guide avoids excessive conceptual explanation that would be better placed in explanation documentation.
1. The guide avoids reference-style content such as long option lists, exhaustive parameter tables, or API descriptions unless directly needed for the task.
1. The guide identifies necessary prerequisites, assumptions, tools, permissions, or starting conditions.
1. Required commands, configuration snippets, file paths, placeholders, and code blocks are formatted correctly.
1. Code blocks declare an appropriate language where the documentation format supports it.
1. Commands, JSON, YAML, Python, shell snippets, or other machine-readable examples are syntactically valid where validation is possible.
1. Placeholders and example values are understandable, usable, and consistent in the context of the task.
1. Branches, alternatives, and optional steps are clearly marked and do not obscure the main path.
1. The guide reaches the practical outcome promised in the title and introduction.
1. The guide includes a simple way to verify successful task completion (e.g., controlling CLI output) and clearly describes the expected results, outputs, or changed system states for the user to recognize success.
1. Risky, destructive, security-sensitive, or irreversible actions are explained before the user performs them.
1. Warning, note, and caution blocks are not too frequent (not more than two per screen) and placed near the relevant steps rather than far away from the action they affect.
1. The guide includes retry, recovery, rollback, or troubleshooting instructions when failure is likely or costly.
1. The document has a valid heading hierarchy, no duplicate top-level titles, and no skipped structural levels. Overall syntax check?
1. Check whether the guide should split into multiple guides for readability.
1. Terminology, product names, command names, UI labels, and placeholder names are used consistently.
1. The language is concise, direct, and oriented toward a user who is trying to complete work, not study a topic.
1. The guide links to explanation, reference, or tutorial material when necessary to provide further information that might be needed by the user, but does that sparingly to minimise breaking the flow.
